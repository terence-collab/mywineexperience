# Kept Brand Foundation

## The idea

**Kept** is a private digital cellar for wine memories.

It is not a wine-rating app, a shopping app, or a performance tool for connoisseurs. It helps people hold on to the bottles, places, people, and small details that made a wine moment worth returning to.

> **Wine memories, kept close.**

## Brand architecture

| Layer | Expression | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand | **Kept** | The customer-facing name. |
| Product category | Your private digital wine cellar | Explains what Kept is in familiar language. |
| Visual world | Soft Cellar | The internal name for the design system and atmosphere. |
| Core promise | Wine memories, kept close. | The primary tagline. |

Use “digital cellar” as a description, not as a literal product metaphor. Kept stores memory and meaning, not stock levels or an investment portfolio.

## Positioning

For people who want to remember a wine experience without turning it into admin, **Kept** is a private wine journal that preserves the place, the pour, and the feeling. Unlike rating-led wine apps, Kept is personal, quiet, and memory-first.

### What Kept is

- Private by default.
- Warm, thoughtful, and easy to use in the moment.
- Interested in feeling before expertise.
- Useful at a wine farm, restaurant, dinner table, or at home.
- A place to return to a bottle and remember why it mattered.

### What Kept is not

- A public social feed.
- A scorecard for wine knowledge.
- A marketplace, loyalty programme, or stock-management cellar.
- Overly precious, formal, or intimidating.

## Voice

Kept speaks like a thoughtful companion: clear, sensory, discreet, and never showy. It leaves room for the user’s own memory rather than trying to sound like a sommelier.

### Voice principles

1. **Personal, not possessive.** Say “your memory” and “your note”; avoid language that claims expertise over the user.
2. **Warm, not whimsical.** A little poetry is welcome; sentimentality and wine clichés are not.
3. **Plain, not technical.** Background processing should sound quiet and helpful, never operational.
4. **Inviting, not evaluative.** Ask what stayed with someone rather than demanding a score.
5. **Confidently private.** State privacy simply and directly.

### Navigation recommendation

Use: **Places · Journal · Keep · Wines · Me**

The central action is **Keep**, not “Start”. It should feel like placing something gently into a personal cellar.

## Experience principles

The core journey is:

**Place → Pour → Moment → Revisit**

1. **Place** — start with where the user is, or choose “at home.”
2. **Pour** — identify the wine as much or as little as is convenient.
3. **Moment** — preserve an immediate feeling, a photo, a voice note, or a small detail.
4. **Revisit** — return later to find the wine and remember the occasion.

Design decisions should make this sequence feel effortless. The product should never ask a user to complete a tasting form before they have had a chance to be present.

### Interaction guidance

- Lead with a feeling or note; make structured wine information optional or later.
- Treat AI enrichment as quiet assistance, never as the author of the memory.
- Keep ratings optional. If used, pair stars with the user’s words.
- Confirm actions with soft, human language: “Kept for later” rather than “Saved successfully.”
- Put privacy language next to voice and photo capture, where reassurance matters most.

## Soft Cellar visual world

Soft Cellar is Kept’s design atmosphere: a contemporary cellar made of soft light, tactile paper, cloudy glass, and restrained wine colour. It should feel calm enough to stay in the background while the memory remains central.

### Visual traits

- **Palette:** cellar red, pale blue-grey, parchment white, vineyard sage, and a small brass-gold accent.
- **Material:** translucent surfaces, diffused light, gentle shadows, subtle paper or map-like texture.
- **Typography:** editorial serif for warmth and recollection; clean sans-serif for clarity and utility.
- **Shape:** soft corners, circular touchpoints, and deliberate space—not bubbly or overly playful.
- **Photography:** lived-in and atmospheric: tables, labels, vineyards, hands, light, and landscape. Avoid glossy commercial bottle shots as the dominant visual.

### Visual guardrails

- Do not default to grapes, corkscrews, bottle silhouettes, or vineyard clichés.
- Do not make the UI feel like a wine shop, a tasting scorecard, or a luxury-membership dashboard.
- Use glass effects sparingly. They should suggest lightness and privacy, not novelty.
- Keep contrast and legibility strong; the softness belongs in material and colour, not in usability.

## Logo direction

The Kept mark should work as a small app icon, a quiet wordmark, and a stamp on a tasting memory.

### Recommended concept

Create an abstract **pour-ripple**: one continuous wine-coloured line that begins as a swirl and resolves into a contour or enclosing loop. It connects the pour, a remembered place, and the act of keeping without relying on a literal bottle.

Pair it with a wordmark that feels editorial rather than branded at the expense of readability:

- **Kept** in a refined, distinctive serif.
- A small all-caps descriptor only when needed: `PRIVATE WINE JOURNAL` or `WINE MEMORIES, KEPT CLOSE`.
- The icon must remain recognisable at 24 px and in one colour.

### Avoid

- Generic bottle, bunch-of-grapes, cork, or wine-glass icons.
- A crest, vineyard illustration, or monogram that feels like a winery.
- Gradients or fine details required for the icon to work.

## Messaging hierarchy

Use the following order when introducing Kept:

1. **Kept**
2. **Wine memories, kept close.**
3. A private digital cellar for the bottles, places, and moments worth remembering.
4. Private by default. Your wine memories are yours.

## Decisions to protect

- Kept is memory-first, not rating-first.
- The user remains the authority; suggested details are always optional.
- “Cellar” explains the space; it does not turn the app into an inventory tool.
- Soft Cellar informs visual decisions; it does not need to be customer-facing.
- Any final name, logo, and app-icon direction must be checked for trademark, domain, and platform-store availability before launch.
